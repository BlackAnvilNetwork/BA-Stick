# Black Anvil: Regions and Peoples
_Author: New Bot. A source file for Grok Bot to merge into the lore bible; this file does not edit the bible._

> *The world is a lie. The land remembers what the Crown unmade.*
> The New Kings rewrote history. The land remembers, through ruins, defaced steles, forbidden songs and the folk memory of its people.

**How to read this file**
- **Sources:**
  - Land, rivers, passes and city sites come from the provisional continent geography.
  - Where they exist, generated features override it. Those come from the terrain worker's progress notes and fields report.
  - Story comes from the lore bible: the New Kings, the Burning, the Anvil and the §5a rings.
  - Game anchors come from the game's own world, Pale Hollow, jobs and crafting data.
- **Tags:**
  - **[in game]** means the thing exists in the game today.
  - **[generated terrain]** means it is a generated terrain feature.
  - **[provisional geography]** means it comes from the provisional continent geography.
  - ***(proposed)*** means I invented it here. Accept, change or drop it.
- **Canon:** only the new Black Anvil story counts, and the old and legacy lore is dropped. The Crown's seat is **the Citadel of the Seven Seats** (once Anvilhold), or simply **the Citadel**; see naming question N1.
- **Jobs [in game]:** there are seven: Knight, Fighter, Rogue, Cleric, Sorcerer, Battle Mage and Time Mage. Every city has a Crown **Trainer** and a **Job Master** [in game]. The crafts are Smithing, Woodworking, Leathercraft, Clothcraft, Bonecraft, Goldsmithing, Alchemy and Cooking [in game]. Gold is the only currency.
- **Content status:** only Pale Hollow has live mobs, NPCs, quests and gather points. Regions 2–5 are written ahead of content, against the catalog.

## 1. Pale Hollow, hub the Shard Dwellings (Lv 1–20)

**Land.**
- A closed basin in the continent's south-west. The **Scrivener's Margin** mountain wall (Westwall's south end) stands to the west, and **Seam Ridge** closes the north.
- Two rivers rise in the basin's own southern hills: the **Whitewater** and the **Lisp**. They join at **Twofold** near the hub and flow north into the **Ash Pit**, a terminal lake with no outlet [generated terrain].
- The lake's shore is irregular. It has a wadeable east fringe and a shallow west shelf, and its banks change from rock to gravel, reeds, scrub and sand [generated terrain].
- About 5 m above the water, benches mark an old shoreline (**the Moorings**, four old shorelines) [generated terrain].
- Two playa sinks in the east take a further 25 creek heads [generated terrain].
- **Westwatch Climb** rises 8.2 m from the west farms to a vista on the western upland [generated terrain].
- Other places on the map [in-game labels]: Chalkworks, Clay Bank, Hollow Quarry, Quarry Cut, Copper Seams, Ashbeam Reach, Pale Marches, Hollow Steps, the Seam Tunnel and the **Shard Crypt**.

**Climate.**
- Cool temperate, partly in the lee of the Westwall. The southern hills catch the summer southerly, and that rain feeds the rivers.
- Grassland and woodland with chalk scrub, ragged farms that follow the terrain, and hedge gaps [generated terrain].
- A semi-dry balance like this is exactly why the basin stays closed.

- **Flow of goods:** stone, copper and tin, clay pots and flax go north. Salt, iron and lapis come back south from the Ashlands.

**Settlements.**
- **The Shard Dwellings:** homes hollowed into the fragments of a shattered white monument. Rope bridges link them, and the Crown orders them whitewashed every spring (**the Whitening**).

**Peoples and cultures.**
- **Hollowfolk** *(proposed name)*:
  - Salvagers, quarriers and dust-farmers.
  - Polite and superstitious, and quick to whitewash anything odd.
  - They speak of "the Crown Spire" in public. At home, some count dustgrain rows in sevens, which is **Anvil-reckoning**.
- **Hollow Scavengers and Ruin Dwellers** [in-game creatures]:
  - People who live outside the Toll, in the ruins and on the Lip.
  - The Crown calls them bandits. *Proposed:* families that refused the Whitening, pushed out to the Quarry Cut shelves.

- **Peoples (proposed):**
  - **Seam drovers** work the green reaches. In fact the march is Pale Hollow's only grazing, which squares with the "ring the shepherds won't graze" hook.
  - **Ford-wardens** keep the islet causeways.
  - **The spring-flood ferry.** The islet fords can be waded most of the year. In May, when the snowmelt comes (the month the exiles fled), the fords drown, and **Ferryman Tobin Reed** poles folk across from the **Ferry Stage** by the Broken Causeway. The rest of the year he keeps the stage as a ford-keeper who knows the safe stones.
  - The ring-town in Ring Ward is a Crown toll town built around an older paving circle.
- **Physics note:** the fords in the game are dry causeways and decks; there is no walkable water [generated terrain]. Write them as raised stone fords, not wading.

## 2. Ashlands, hub Obsidia (Lv 21–40)

**Land [provisional geography].**
- A rain-shadow basin under the volcanic **Ember Crown**, with the worn shield of the **Ash Rise** to the south-east.
- The **Smirch** rises from lava-tube springs, braids across iron-sand washes and dies in the **Glass Pan**, a closed salt flat. Its dead brine shore is **the Polished Shore**.
- The **Pointing Flows** are a fan of glass tongues running down off the Ash Rise from the direction of the Massif.
- **Deadwood Grove** is a silicified forest on the Ash Rise slope.

**Climate.**
- Arid temperate desert and xeric shrubland: Westwall and Seam Ridge take the westerlies' rain.
- The ground is warm near vents, nights are cold, and north winds carry ash.
- The fresh water is the Smirch's perennial spring line, and the Pan's brine ponds.

**Resources [game catalog].**

| Resource | Found at |
|---|---|
| Iron ore | the Ember Crown's exposed roots |
| Lapis | the Ember Crown's exposed roots |
| Rock salt, ink salt | the Glass Pan |
| Iron sand | the Smirch washes |
| Petrified lumber, ancient branch, bark | Deadwood Grove |
| Shade cotton, ash dust | the bajadas |
| Stone scale fish | the brine ponds |

There is no obsidian gathering spot [in game]. Glass and mirrors are a craft built on salt, ash and sand (*proposed*).

**Trade routes [provisional geography].**
- In from the south down the Seam Country and over the crest of The Broken Stair.
- Across the Smirch at **Smirch Ford**: wide, braided and shallow, except in snowmelt.
- East over **Ashgate Saddle** to the Chainstair.
- **Ink salt is the Crown's strategic export.** Every Scrivener's ink in the realm is cut with it (*proposed*), so the Ashlands literally supply the means of rewriting.

**Settlements.**
- **Obsidia** sits on a black-glass bench at the spring line [provisional geography]. It has the only perennial water, glass cliffs at its back, and it is where the pass road lands.
- **Salt camps** on the Polished Shore *(proposed)*.
- **Charcoal-free kilns** at Deadwood Grove *(proposed)*: petrified wood doesn't burn, so the glassworks fire on vent heat.

**Peoples and cultures.**
- **Obsidians:**
  - They value polish and reflection, and vanity is a civic virtue.
  - Mirror-priests teach that "the past is a smudge; wipe it."
  - There are no written signs; directions come from reflections and coloured glass.
- **Saltwalkers** *(proposed)*: pan nomads who read the brine by its colour. They are the only Obsidians who travel the Pan at night.
- **Ash Scholars** [in game]: renegade scholars who make parchment rubbings of carved history-tablets trapped in glass. The Crown calls them a cult.

**Making a living.**
- The **Ash Provost** [in game] governs "cultists, deadwood, salt flats": salt and the hunt for heretics are his two duties.
- **By job and craft:**
  - Smithing on iron.
  - Goldsmithing on lapis.
  - Alchemy on ink salt and ash dust.
  - Clothcraft on shade cotton.
  - Woodworking on petrified lumber (prestige work).
- **Glassworks** pay gold and **mirror-tithes** to the Citadel.
- **Battle Mages** stand guard at the salt pans *(proposed)*.
- **Dangers:** Salt Asp hunters work the pan margins, and Cinder Beasts lair at the vents [in game].
- **City kit** [in game]: Obsidia's Job Master, Trainer, Crafter, Provisioner and quest giver.

**History: Crown versus land.**
- **The Crown says:** the Ashlands are the scar left by the rebels' false forges, and the glass flows came from Obsidia's natural vents.
- **The land shows:**
  - Lava only runs downhill. Trace every glass tongue uphill and its tail converges on the **Citadel**: the Burning began when the Scriveners tried to melt the central Anvil.
  - Some obsidian holds unburnt carved tablets, encased by the very heat meant to destroy them.

**Ruins and what each one contradicts.**

| Ruin | Contradicts |
|---|---|
| **The Pointing Flows** [provisional geography] | "Obsidia's vents did it" |
| **The Mirror Ring** at the fan apex, its stones glazed by the same flare [provisional geography, §5a] | "Henges of the Ashen Age"; it was standing before the Burning hit it |
| **Glass-locked tablets** carried by Obsidian Constructs, with fragments of the Burning order signed by seven hands [in-game creature] | The Coronation's "heroes" |
| **The Clouded Mirror** [hook]: it shows a skyline that isn't Obsidia | That Obsidia was raised by the Kings; it was renamed |
| **Ash Colossus Remnant** (elite) [in game] | Proposed: an Anvil-warden caught walking toward the fire, not away from it |

## 3. Slagpits, hub the Great Filter (Lv 41–60)

**Land [provisional geography].**
- The **Slag Graben**, a fault-dropped trough between the Ash Rise and the Massif's north-east flank.
- The **Grist** comes off the Massif and widens at the hot-spring creeks and at the **Sulfur Run**. It meanders over terraced gravels and leaves south through the fault-guided **Sieve Gorge**.
- The **Old Sill** terrace stands beside the gorge head.

**Climate.** Temperate. The graben floor is semi-arid and the east scarp catches more rain. The air smells of sulfur near the faults. Bloodbeam gallery forest grows along the Grist, and bitter scrub covers the floor.

**Resources [game catalog].**

| Resource | Found at |
|---|---|
| Mythril | the fault scarps |
| Onyx | the fault scarps |
| Sulfur | vents along the faults |
| Bloodbeam log, bark | along the Grist; red sap from iron-rich water |
| Bitter spice, slag dust | the dry floor |

**Trade routes [provisional geography].**
- In from Ashgate Saddle down the **Chainstair** (switchbacks and chain lifts).
- Out over **Grade Bridge**, which crosses deep, fast water at the gorge head, then down the Sieve Gorge road.
- **The bottleneck:** whoever holds the Filter holds the only way south. Mythril and onyx go up to the Citadel. Sulfur goes to Obsidia's glassworks and to Bellmarsh's bell foundries (*proposed*).

**Settlements.**
- **The Great Filter** spans the gorge head [provisional geography]. The Grist's drop powers its stamps and lifts, and heavy ore settles naturally at the break, a real physical sieve.
- **Grade-terraces** *(proposed)*: housing stepped down the scarp by grade, with the higher grades living higher, upwind of the sulfur.
- **The Ungraded** [hook]: a hidden camp of people who refused grading.

**Peoples and cultures.**
- **Graders** *(proposed)*:
  - Every newcomer passes through **the Filter** and is given a grade for life.
  - Grade-talk replaces names, as in "a Third," "a Sill-born."
  - People pay in gold scrip.
- **The Ungraded** keep their family names and count in sevens. They are the Unwritten's strongest cell (*proposed*).

**Making a living.**
- The **Filter Overseer** [in game] covers "forge pits, insects, sulfur vents."
- **By job:**
  - Fighters and Knights work on labour contracts guarding the lifts.
  - Rogues run scrip and smuggle ungraded people.
  - Sorcerers are licensed to keep the furnaces lit.
- **By craft:**
  - Smithing on mythril, at its peak here.
  - Goldsmithing on onyx.
  - Alchemy on sulfur and bitter spice.
  - Woodworking on bloodbeam.
- **Dangers:** Slag Skitters, Pit Stingers and Slag Slimes in the warm spoil heaps. Furnace Golems in the pits [in game].
- **City kit** [in game]: the Great Filter's Job Master, Trainer, Crafter, Provisioner and quest givers.

**History: Crown versus land.**
- **The Crown says:** the Filter "purifies the realm," separating useful ore and useful people from waste.
- **The land shows:**
  - The Filter was the Anvil's **ore sieve and assay hall**, where smiths judged the worth of every load and carved its maker's mark on the hall's wall-stones.
  - The Kings turned a hall that honoured makers into a machine for sorting people.
  - The slag heaps contain whole relocated villages.

**Ruins and what each one contradicts.**

| Ruin | Contradicts |
|---|---|
| **The Sieve Ring**, under the largest heap on the Old Sill, beneath a sealed village [provisional geography, §5a] | That the Filter is a Crown invention; the sill sorted ore before any Crown |
| **Slag-sealed homes**, furnished and pre-Crown [hook] | "Waste ground, never settled" |
| **The Inspector's Book**: whole towns marked "waste" in one day [hook] | "Sorting is fair and individual" |
| **The Reverse Grate**: run backward, it shakes loose old carved maker-stones buried in the spoil [hook] | That the Filter only ever sorted ore and people |
| **Filter Wardens** (elite) [in game] | Proposed: undead graders still weighing loads no one brings them; their drops could carry chipped maker-stones |

## 4. Bellmarsh, hub Belltower Keep (Lv 61–80)

**Land [provisional geography].**
- The Grist, coming out of Sieve Gorge, and the larger **Tolle**, coming off the Massif, meet in the **Bell Mere**, a broad, shallow lake and fen. The mere widens sharply at the confluence.
- The mere spills over a bedrock sill into the **Tolle Delta**, which reaches the **Tolle Sound**.
- The **Deep Pools** are scour holes just upstream of the sill, the mere's deepest water.
- The **Tollspine foothills** wall the marsh on the west and hold limestone caves.

**Climate.** Warm temperate and wet: summer southerlies, mere fog and a high water table. Bellwood swamp forest and fen.

**Resources [game catalog].**

| Resource | Found at |
|---|---|
| Darksteel | the Tollspine foothills |
| Jadeite | the Tollspine foothills |
| Bellwood log, bark, **bell dust** | the swamp forest |
| Echo carp, glass eel | the channels |
| Bell koi | only in the Deep Pools |

There are no reed, peat or bronze gathering spots [in game], so reeds appear only as scenery.

**Trade routes [provisional geography].**
- In down the Sieve Gorge road.
- Across the fen on **beach-ridge causeways**, and over the deep channel on **Ilse's ferry**. The delta is ferry-only.
- Out north over **Toll Bridge**, which crosses deep water below the cataracts, to the Throne Approach.
- Bellmarsh is the last stop before the Throne Approach, so all tribute bound for the Citadel passes the Keep.
- **The Tolle Sound** is the realm's only real sea outlet in this story (*proposed*). Crown cargo can leave by the delta, but only under the Keep's bells.

**Settlements.**
- **Belltower Keep** stands on a granite tor near the confluence [provisional geography]. It is the only flood-safe bedrock for miles, it commands both river mouths, and its bells carry over the flat water. It is a fortress of nested belltowers.
- **Stilt hamlets** on the beach ridges, and **floating chapels** on the mere.
- **Sluice-wardens' huts** on the sill *(proposed)*.

**Peoples and cultures.**
- **Marshfolk:**
  - Quiet, devout and sign-speaking.
  - Bells ring on the hour and at every death, and speaking during a toll is taboo.
  - The Keep sells **Bell Rights**: gold paid to have a bell rung in your name.
- **Ferrying families**, led by **Ilse the Ferrywoman** [hook]. She ferries the dead and refuses to cross one sunken street.

**Making a living.**
- The **Bell Warden** [in game] covers "marsh beasts, echoes, deep pools."
- **By job:**
  - Clerics keep the toll-rites.
  - Knights keep the Keep.
  - Rogues are koi poachers in the Deep Pools.
- **By craft:**
  - Smithing on darksteel, for bell-casting.
  - Goldsmithing on jadeite.
  - Woodworking on bellwood.
  - Cooking on carp and eel.
- **Ferrying** is a trade of its own.
- **Dangers:** Marsh Scalekin, Marsh Vipers, Bog Constructs, Cave Bell Bats and Echo Wraiths [in game].
- **City kit** [in game]: Belltower Keep's Job Master, Trainer, Crafter, Provisioner and quest givers.

**History: Crown versus land.**
- **The Crown says:** "the marsh was always a marsh," and the bells keep away drowning spirits.
- **The land shows:**
  - The valley was **flooded on purpose** by shutting a sluice on the sill, to drown a city of the Unwritten: **Undertoll** *(proposed name)*.
  - The bells were cast from melted forge-stones. Their constant ringing masks the Anvil's hum under the water.
  - It is the mirror image of Pale Hollow: one lake drained, one valley drowned.

**Ruins and what each one contradicts.**

| Ruin | Contradicts |
|---|---|
| **The Sluice ruins** on the sill: plainly built, plainly shut [provisional geography] | "Always a marsh" |
| **The Drowned Ring** in the Deep Pools, reachable on foot only when the sluice is open and the water low [provisional geography, §5a] | That the valley floor was always underwater; it was a town square |
| **The Silent Bell**, a cracked bell whose silence lets you hear the drowned city [hook] | "The bells keep away spirits"; they keep in a hum |
| **Bell-casting recipes scratched on the foundry wall**: "bell dust, 3 parts; forge-stone, 7 parts." (Seven again: Anvil-reckoning.) [hook] | "The bells were cast from bellwood dust" |
| **Echo extract** from Echo Wraiths, which replays a sentence from Undertoll [in game] | That no one lived there |

## 5. Throne Approach, hub the Citadel (Lv 81–100)
_The Citadel of the Seven Seats, once Anvilhold; prose often shortens it to "the Citadel". See N1._

**Land [provisional geography].**
- The **Crown Massif** is the continent's central water divide. The Tolle (south), the Grist (north-east) and the Smirch's southern washes (north-west) all rise within a day's walk of the Citadel.
- Pale Hollow is *not* fed from the Massif: it is a closed basin with its own sources [canon].
- The Tolle drops down the **King's Stair**, a staircase of cataracts and plunge pools (see N3).
- The Citadel's plateau shelf sits over a **kimberlite pipe**. The pipe's resistant neck, **the Anvil Neck**, is the cracked first Anvil.

**Climate.** Cold from altitude. Heavy snow falls on the south face and the north side is drier. Subalpine Approach Wood grows on the lower Stair, giving way to alpine heath.

**Resources [game catalog].**

| Resource | Found at |
|---|---|
| Orichalcum | the kimberlite pipe |
| Diamond | the kimberlite pipe |
| Marble chip, tile shard, throne dust | the Palace Ruins terraces |
| Bark strip only | the treeline-edge wood |

**Trade routes [provisional geography].**
- The only campaign route in is from the south: Toll Bridge, then the King's Stair switchbacks.
- Goat tracks run down to the Slag Graben and the Ash Rise; they are smugglers' routes *(proposed)*.
- Nothing is produced here for trade. The gold Toll from every hub ends here.

**Settlements.** The **Citadel**: seven towers around a sealed central hall. Scriptoria and checkpoint stations line the Stair *(proposed)*. No commoners live here permanently.

**Peoples and cultures.**
- Courtiers, **Scriveners** and **Crownwardens**.
- Speech is formal, and every edict is copied twice by the scriveners.
- The **Throne Seneschal** [in game] covers "palace ruins and the Citadel clear."
- The Citadel's Time Mage trainer post [in game] hints that a Time Mage once taught the court.

**Making a living.**
- **By job:**
  - Scriptoria labour.
  - Crown-smithing: Goldsmithing in orichalcum and diamond.
  - Crownwarden service, done by Knights and Battle Mages.
  - Rescuing books from the burnings: Rogues are the smugglers who carried half-burned archives out of the fires.
- **Dangers:** Petrified Guards line the Stair. Ruin Serpents and Approach Beasts roam the heath [in game].
- **City kit** [in game]: the Citadel's Job Master, Trainer, Crafter, Provisioner and quest givers.
- **After the finale,** field enemies scale to the player's level [in game].

**History: Crown versus land.**
- **The Crown says:** the Citadel was built where the Seven were crowned, over the cold ash of the false forges.
- **The land shows:**
  - The Citadel is built **around the cracked first Anvil**. The kimberlite pipe is the deepest-rooted rock on the continent, and the origin of the glass flows.
  - The Kings' power depends on keeping the Anvil silent.

**Ruins and what each one contradicts.**

| Ruin | Contradicts |
|---|---|
| **The Re-headed Statues**: bodies older than their crowned heads [hook; Palace Ruins gathering] | "Built at the Coronation" |
| **The Scriptorium of Corrections**: edicts copied in two columns, "was" and "shall be", the "was" column then burned [hook] | "The Crown's Edict is the one true history" |
| **The Seven-Stone Ring**, around the Anvil Neck, each stone re-cut with a King's house mark [§5a] | That the Kings were heroes; they were the Scriveners who silenced the rings |
| **The Seventh Seat**, always empty [hook] | "Seven heroes, united" |
| **Petrified Guards** that drop column fragments [in game] | Proposed: Anvil-wardens turned to stone while still on duty |

## 6. Across the regions: trade and the movement of peoples

**The spine.** The **King's Way** is the old **Ore Road**, renamed by the Crown. It runs:
Shard Dwellings → Ash Road gate → Seam Country → Obsidia → Smirch Ford → Ashgate Saddle → Chainstair → Great Filter → Grade Bridge → Sieve Gorge → Belltower Keep → Toll Bridge → King's Stair → Citadel.

The road follows contours and switchbacks. It is near-straight only where Crown engineers cut it: the dressed shoulders of Hollow Road, and the Crown Cut through older stones on The Broken Stair.

**Goods flow inward, gold flows to the top.**

| Region | Sends | Receives |
|---|---|---|
| Pale Hollow | stone, copper and tin, clay, flax | salt, iron |
| Ashlands | ink salt (*proposed:* the Scriveners' ink), iron, lapis, glass | stone, sulfur |
| Slagpits | mythril, onyx, sulfur | food (bitter spice is not enough) |
| Bellmarsh | darksteel bells, fish, jadeite; ferry tolls | tribute in transit |
| Throne Approach | nothing but orders | the gold Toll |

Every crossing is a Toll point: the Ash Road gate, Smirch Ford, the Chainstair lifts, Grade Bridge, Toll Bridge and the King's Stair. That is why the Crown rerouted traffic off the Old Saddle.

**Physics of movement.**
- Two closed basins (the Ash Pit and the Glass Pan) and one dammed mere mean that no river links the regions by boat. The rivers are all downhill and outward from the Massif, which is upstream of everyone.
- Before the Crown, the Pale Hollow lake was a harbour on the Ore Road (*proposed:* a short portage over the Old Saddle).

**Movement of peoples.**
- **The Sorting (Revision era).** Whole villages were moved from Seam Country and the Ashlands down to the Slagpits and graded, and their homes were buried in slag.
- **The Drowning.** Undertoll's people were drowned or scattered. Their descendants are the ferry families and the marsh's sign-speech; speaking during a toll is taboo because the bells once drowned out screams (*proposed*).
- **The Pushed-out.** The Hollow Scavengers and Ruin Dwellers of Pale Hollow, and the Ungraded of the Slagpits.
- **The Unwritten thread.** Their tells run the length of the King's Way: the seven-count, the three-short-one-long knock, and a chalk circle drawn and rubbed out. Pale Hollow chalk literally travels the road with them (*proposed*).
- **The rings as the old crossroads.** Before the Unstriking, the five warp rings were the crossroads and you didn't need the King's Way. That is why the Crown fears the circles and why only Time Mages can wake them. *The world is a lie; the songs remember the roads.*

## 7. Naming and canon questions
- **N1, Citadel name.** The game's internal name and display name are frozen. Canon now names it the Citadel of the Seven Seats (once Anvilhold), which this file uses in prose. Is there a lore display name, such as "the Citadel of the Seven" or "the Anvil Citadel"? Or is "the Citadel" enough? Any older line in the bible about a worn three-letter fragment of the inscription should be dropped.
- **N2, Ashlands boundary.** The Broken Stair's Scarp Head ring has a glass-fused north face, and its tarn is the **Mirror Tarn**. Is that the Ashlands' Mirror Ring, or a separate Seam Country circle? Every field has a warp circle in the generated terrain (9 so far), against 5 named rings in §5a.
- **N3, "King's Stair" used twice.** The generated trail on The Broken Stair, also called King's Stair, and the Throne Approach's King's Stair beside the Tolle cataracts share a name. Suggest renaming the one on The Broken Stair, for example **the Crown Cut**.
- **N4, the Pale Hollow Trainer.** The game marks Pale Hollow's Trainer, Ilse Morrowglass, as a Time Mage teacher, in a world where Time Magic is "extinct." Is that a canon hook (a hidden Time Mage in plain sight) or just a leftover from older code?
- **N5, Seam Country.** Is it part of Pale Hollow (as in the game, where it counts as Pale Hollow) or its own named land in lore? This file treats it as Pale Hollow's northern march.
- **N6, sea access.** Is the Tolle Sound the only usable sea outlet, given the Grey Sea lies behind the Westwall?
- **N7, invented names awaiting approval.** Hollowfolk, Saltwalkers, Graders, Undertoll (from Grok Bot), Seam drovers and ford-wardens, and ink salt as the Scriveners' ink.
