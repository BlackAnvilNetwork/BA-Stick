## 0. Canon guardrails

- **Dark fantasy, nothing else (Macks, top rule).** Black Anvil is a story of usurpation, grief, faith, craft, betrayal, memory and defiance. Nothing in it is a metaphor for any real-world technology, and no part of the story starts from one. The Kings won by edict, fire, chisel and fear; the land, the stones, the songs and the people remember.
- **One story.** Black Anvil's history is the only history. There is no older game world to reconcile against.
- **The core theme is a stolen history.** The New Kings usurped Anvilhold, burned the makers' books, chiselled their names off the stones and made it a crime to say them. Every place has two stories: the Kings' version (the **Book of Crowns**) and what the land, the stones and the people remember. The player learns to tell them apart.
- **Rule of evidence.** Struck stone is always true. Ink can lie. Witnesses are true but incomplete. Every fragment the player finds is one of these three kinds.
- **Gold is the only currency.** The Kings tax it as the **Toll**.
- **In-world names only.** No code ids appear in this bible. The Kings' seat is **the Citadel**. Its lore name is **the Citadel of the Seven Seats**: the seven-tower core of **Anvilhold**, the fallen makers' city, now overrun by the Cultists of the Seven (§11). The Kings rule hidden in its sealed Anvil Hall. The game's current display name is a code matter; prose never uses it.
- **Not built yet = not shown working.** Guilds appear only as a light epilogue hint. Warp rings are physical landmarks with no wired interaction yet. Waking a ring brightening the sky is lore only, with no render commitment.
- **Tone:** dark fantasy under bright pixels. Quiet dread, concrete detail, short lines, dry humour, no memes, no fourth-wall breaks. See §23.

## 1. Creation myth and cosmology

### 1.1 The Struck Faith's telling: "The First Strike"
Before anything, there was the **Unstruck**: a cold dark where nothing had happened, because nothing had yet been *made*. Things flickered into being and out again, and nothing stayed true for longer than it was watched.

Then came the **Smith**, who has no name because she was there before names. She found a stone that would hold a shape, and she called it the **Anvil**. She found a stone that would make a shape, and she called it the **Hammer**. She struck once, and the sound of that strike was the first true thing. Where the sound went, the world stayed. The sound is still going; the edge of the world is just the place it has reached so far.

The sparks of the first strike flew up and did not come down. They are the **Anvil's Sparks**, the pale band across the night sky (§14). Each spark is a deed that can't be undone.

The Smith did not keep the Hammer. She **laid it crosswise on the Anvil** and walked away, so that anyone could take it up. That is the *open strike*, and it is the oldest law: *work struck once, honestly, by your own hand, is true for all who use it, and the Hammer belongs to no one hand.* Smiths still swear it: ***Struck once, true for all.***

The Smith left two children to watch over the world:
- the **Witness**, who watches and remembers (the moon is her lamp), and
- the **Keeper of the Glass**, who turns the hourglass so that what was made keeps its shape (the three swirls in the night sky are her hourglasses, the **Turning Wheels**).

Everything the Smith struck became the land. The first Anvil sank into the high centre of the continent, and its weight raised the **Crown Massif**. Its hum runs through the rock as the **deep hum** that forge-stones still carry.

### 1.2 The Crown Rite's telling: the *Book of Ash*
The Book of Crowns opens with the *Book of Ash*. Before the Crown there was only **Ash**: a grey waste of clans, plague and "false forges" that hammered lies into stone. The **Flame** ran wild over everything. Seven heroes walked into the fire and **quenched** it with their own hands. Where their hands touched the ash, it hardened into land, and the Flame, grateful to be tamed, crowned them. "Before the Crown there was only Ash. After the Crown there is Order."

The *Book of Ash* never mentions a Smith or a Hammer. Anvils appear only as the tools of the false forges.

### 1.3 Cosmology (shared facts both sides agree on)
- The world is the **Reach**: one continent, ringed by grey sea, under a sky with a sun, one moon, a pale band of stars, three spiral swirls, and frequent falling stars.
- **Stone remembers.** Certain stones, the **forge-stones**, keep what is struck into them and hum when touched. Everyone knows this; the two faiths disagree on whether it is holy or dangerous.
- **The dead linger if forgotten.** Both faiths have to explain the undead. The Crown blames rebellion; the Struck Faith blames erasure (§12).
- **Time can be read.** Some people see a thing as it was first made. The Crown calls it heresy; the old world called it Time Magic.

## 3. The New Kings

### 3.1 Who they are
The **New Kings** are six crowned rulers who sit on the **Seven-Seat Throne** in the Citadel. The Book of Crowns says there are seven. The seventh seat is always empty, and court custom says it is "kept for the realm." Officially the Kings are elected by "the Concord of the Realm"; in practice a seat passes only by appointment of the surviving Kings.

**Their faces are never shown.** Only their **crowns** are seen, carried on poles through every hub. Portraits, coins and statues show crowns over blank or re-carved heads. Whether the Kings are six mortal lines, the same six since the Coronation, or something that has outlived being people at all is never answered, and canon keeps it so (§19).

### 3.2 How they rule
- **The Book of Crowns**: the one sanctioned history and holy book, one bound copy per hub, bolted to a lectern. Pages are torn out and replaced, never added.
- **The Scriveners and the Scriptorium** (§5): write, amend and re-cut.
- **The Crownwardens**: soldiery, road guards and ring-breakers.
- **The Toll of Gold**: gold taken at every hub gate, road toll and bridge. All of it flows up the King's Way to the Citadel.
- **The Heralds**: in every hub a Campaign Herald reads the Book of Crowns aloud and hands out "Crown service" (the quests).
- **Centralised training**: one Crown-licensed Trainer per city sells every job's abilities on the official curriculum, so that no discipline keeps its own teachers or its own history.
- **Grading, bells and whitewash**: each region is held by its own instrument (§§7–11).

**Their creed:** *"Before the Crown there was only Ash."*
**Their method:** they don't burn the past; they **edit** it. Erasure gets noticed; revision becomes permanent.
**Their fear:** the Anvil. A world where makers sign their own work and the stones keep their names is a world they can't rule.

**House Unnamed.** The Seventh House was **House Tallowmere**, whose head, Hesk Tallowmere, was the seventh Scrivener. Hesk refused the Unstriking, then refused the crown. The other six burned the House out of every book and painted its arms out white. Hesk left the Hammer laid crosswise on the Anvil, walked out of the Citadel, and became the first of the Unwritten (§5.3). The Book of Crowns cannot admit there were only six, so it keeps a white shield and an empty seat.

**Sieveholt's headless hammer.** The only hammer in Crown heraldry has no haft: a hammer no hand can hold.

### 4.1 The defaced Anvil arms (pre-Crown)
*Original:* per fess azure and earth-brown; an **anvil** with a **ring of seven stones** above it and a **hammer laid crosswise** (the open strike). Supporters: a **smith** and an **hourglass-bearer**. No crown; a **ring of linked hands** instead. Motto: ***"Struck once, true for all."***

*How the Kings defaced it,* on every ruin in the Reach:
- The ring of hands was chiselled into a crown's points.
- The hammer was scraped away, so the anvil reads as an empty altar.
- The seven stones were re-cut as seven stars, now read as "the Seven Kings."
- The hourglass-bearer's face was hacked off (the Glass Purge); the smith was re-carved kneeling.
- The motto was overcut: *"Struck once, by the Crown."*

The scraped-off hammer is the campaign's long clue (§§18–19).

## 5. The Scriveners and the Unwritten

### 5.1 The Seven Scriveners (history)
Seven families kept the Anvil's chronicles: Vellance, Carrow, Mirrane, Sieveholt, Tollund, Ashgrave, Tallowmere. Their craft was honest for centuries: writing, singing, teaching. The rot began when people trusted books over stones. Six families became Kings. The seventh vanished from every book.

### 5.2 The Scriptorium (today)
The Scriveners' order in the Crown era. Copy-houses in every hub; the **Scriptorium of Corrections** in the Citadel, where the great books have two columns: *"was"* and *"shall be."*
- **Correctors** travel with chisel, whitewash and a sealed book of approved wordings.
- **Ranks:** Copyist → Corrector → Reviser → Archivist of the Seat.
- **Marks:** ink-black fingers (a mark of pride), grey cords at the wrist, one per decade of service.
- **Saying:** *"Ink is kind. Stone is stubborn."*

### 5.3 The Unwritten
A scattering of families and lone witnesses who remember what supposedly never happened, and pass it mouth to ear. Not a guild, not an army.
- **Founder:** Hesk Tallowmere, the refused seventh Scrivener.
- **Signs:** counting in sevens (Anvil-reckoning); the **knock** (three short, one long: "the strike"); a chalk circle of seven marks drawn and rubbed out at once.
- **Creed:** *"Remember for those who cannot."*
- **Practice:** each member "keeps a song": memorises a stretch of true history as a song, often a single town's, word for word, and teaches it to one heir.
- **Aims:** shelter hidden Time Mages; recover true runes; lay the erased dead to rest; one day, take up the Hammer.
- **Weakness:** witnesses are true but incomplete. No Unwritten knows the whole story. That is why the player's trail matters.

- **The Ungraded**: Slagpits refusers who keep family names (§9).
- **The Halls of the Eightfold**: Mayflower's eight craft halls, founded by the Exodus of Anvilhold. Keepers of the Carried Marks and the hidden crosswise hammer (§7.6). Lore only; no hall or guild system exists in game.

- **The Silent Choir**: Bellmarsh women who sign the drowned city's prayers during bell tolls, when speech is forbidden (§10).
- **Guilds**: small, newly re-forming bands of adventurers. The Crown tolerates them as useful; the Unwritten watch them as the first free companies the Reach has seen in three hundred years. (Epilogue hint only; see §20.)

## 7. Pale Hollow (Lv 1–20) — Chapter I
Hub: **the Shard Dwellings**, whose scout encampment is the gate of **Mayflower** (§7.6). Fields: the Pale Hollow basin and, in Seam Country, Hollow Road, Forest Basin, Spur Road, Ring Ward, Islet Fords, Green Reach, Coil Pass and Coil Ridge. The only region with live content.

**The land.** A pale, chalky, fog-soft basin. A mountain wall on the west (**the Scrivener's Margin**). Two rivers, **the Whitewater** and **the Lisp**, rise in the southern hills, join at **Twofold** near the hub, and run north into the **Ash Pit**, a round lake with no outlet inside a pale chalk rim. A low **Seam Ridge** closes the north; the Ash Road leaves over it. Pale Hollow is a closed basin: no water reaches it from the Crown Massif.

**The Shard.** A colossal shattered white monument. Its fragments have been hollowed into homes, and people live inside it.
- *Official:* the **Crown Spire**, toppled by rebels in the Ashen Age.
- *Truth:* an **name tower** of the Anvil. Under the whitewash its faces are covered in struck names: births, marriages, masterworks, all pre-Crown.

### 7.1 People and customs
**Hollowfolk**: salvagers, quarriers, dust-farmers. Polite, superstitious, quick to whitewash anything odd.
- **The Whitening.** Every spring, by Crown order, every wall is whitewashed. Children get a day off to help. It's the region's biggest festival, and its quiet sorrow: grandmothers whitewash slowly.
- **Dialect.** Soft, clipped, lots of "mind." *"Mind the crack"* means both "watch your step" and "don't look too closely."
- **Sayings:** *"White hides, white hides."* · *"Dust in the bread, dust in the bed."* · *"Count your rows, not your elders."*
- **Food.** Dustgrain flatbread baked on hot limestone (always slightly gritty with pale dust); fossil-minnow soup; boar cooked in clay; antidote-root tea for bad water. Feast dish: **Whitening loaf**, a chalk-white crust split to show dark grain inside.
- **Burial.** The Crown way: a bell, then the name retired from the Book of Crowns and the body laid in the Shard Crypt. The Hollow way, done quietly: the family scratches the dead person's name inside a shard wall where the whitewash won't reach, and touches it once a year.

### 7.3 Places and hooks
- **The Peeling Wall**: a shard home where rain has washed the whitewash away, showing a pre-Crown birth carving: children's names struck into the shard.
- **The Humming Stone**: the **Pale Dust Crystal** near the Shard Crypt hums when struck. The first Anvil echo the player hears.
- **The Terrace Keeper's tallies**: counts dustgrain rows in sevens. *"My mother counted this way, and hers."*
- **Whitening Orders**: a Crownwarden posting demanding re-whitening of "the north faces only."
- **The Shard Crypt**: home of the **Shard Wights**, the Glass Purge's dead (§12).
- **The Chalk Ring**: the region's warp ring, a sunken rune-floor at the old crossroads that Crown maps label **The Sheepfold** (84,216). Pale Hollow has no herders; nobody asks whose fold it was (§13).
- **NPC voices.** The **Shard Elder**, Old Orrin Haltmark, tells the Crown Spire story in public with perfect sincerity, and keeps the true one at home (§7.6). The **Crypt Watch** gives "Shut the Mouth," killing Wights as undead. The **Campaign Herald** reads the Book of Crowns.

### 7.5 Seam Country, the northern march (still part of Pale Hollow)
Between Seam Ridge and the Ember Crown foothills, with the Westwall on the west, lies **Seam Country**: Pale Hollow's northern march and the second half of Chapter I. It is not a separate region in lore; it's the hollow's frontier, where the Crown's roads are newest and the old circles show most plainly. Westerlies spill through the **Coil Gap**, so the land is wetter in the west and north. The **Seam Water** drains east toward the Smirch.

**Peoples.**
- **Seam drovers**: Pale Hollow's only herders, working the green reaches. They will not graze the Ungrazed Ring. *"Grass that grows in bands isn't for eating."*
- **Ford-wardens**: keep the raised stone fords and causeways of the islets. Families bury their own at the West Cairn with old ferry coins.
- **Ring-towners**: Crown toll-town folk of Ring Ward, whose walls bend around a paving circle nobody dared cut.

**The circles.** Every field zone has at least one sunken circle of rune-stones. Only one of them, the **Chalk Ring** (The Sheepfold), is one of the five great rings. The others are **lesser circles**: waystones that carried the great rings' song across the land. They're smaller, they never had their own Time Mage keepers, and they can't be woken on their own (§13). Forest Basin's three lesser circles are the Kettle Ring, the Scraped Ring and the Spurgate Ring.

### 7.6 Mayflower, the exiles' town (Anvilhold's daughter)
The hub pad of the Shard Dwellings (the Shard Dwellings) is the old **scout encampment** on Pale Hollow's eastern spur. To a new arrival it looks like a camp. It's the gate of **Mayflower**, the crafting town that Anvilhold's exiles built into the Shard's eastern fragments, and the most impressive thing in Pale Hollow once you see it whole. The name comes from the flight itself: **the exiles left Anvilhold in May** and came down into a valley under the Shard that was full of wildflowers, so they called their town **Mayflower**. The Reach has no old month names of its own in this bible; "May" is the month as the exiles named it. *Bank the fires low* is still the town's first rule, but it is a custom, not the source of the name. A Crown rider on the Ash Road should see a little smoke from a salvage camp, not the glow of fifty forges.

#### The Exodus of Anvilhold (CR 0–3)
**Why they fled.** Before the Coronation, Anvilhold, the town around the first Anvil, was a city of smiths, makers and crafters. Every masterwork was struck into a forge-stone so the maker's name would last. When the six crowned themselves on the cracked Anvil (CR 0), the makers understood what came next: a crown that rules by editing cannot let anyone sign their own work. Hesk Tallowmere's refusal was the signal. In May of the Crown's first year, about two thousand people left in one spring night, most of them crafters.

**What they carried.**
- **Tools.** Each family took its own hammer, tongs and stamps. A maker's tools are still their only inheritance.
- **The Carried Marks.** Maker's marks rubbed onto thin copper sheets from the Anvil's own stones before the Correctors reached them: the oldest unaltered names outside stone.
- **The hammer motif.** Every maker stamped a tiny **crosswise hammer** beside their mark, the open strike the Kings were scraping from every crest. It's still there, hidden on the underside of Mayflower work. Beside it many makers add a small **hawthorn sprig**, the may blossom, as a minor second emblem.

**The road.** The King's Stair was guarded, so the exiles went off the Massif by goat tracks, west across the Ember Crown's shoulder, and down through what is now Seam Country into Pale Hollow. **They walked the fragment trail in reverse.** The player, reading it in order (the First Fragment to the Thirteenth Fragment), is following the exodus home toward Anvilhold.
- **Ember Crown shoulder.** They lit a signal fire for stragglers. The **Crown Stones** (the Eleventh Fragment) were already aimed at that summit beacon. The exiles kept it lit through that spring, and it has not burned since.
- **The Scarp.** At **Scarp Head Ring** (the Thirteenth Fragment) the elders laid their hands on the old ring. In the **Scarp Cave** (the Twelfth Fragment) they sheltered beside the Scriveners' Oath that Hesk had struck there. Correctors re-cut six signatures into crowns decades later but would not touch the hammer.
- **Seam Country.** They rested at the lesser circles (Islet Ring, Older Paving and the Seven Posts: the Ninth, Eighth and Fifth Fragments) because rings were the only places the Crown's new maps didn't claim yet.
- **Into the hollow.** Over the **Old Saddle** (the Fourth Fragment) by the Ore Road ruts; the Ash Road toll gate wasn't built until the Raising. Past the Moorings (the Second Fragment), where they counted themselves in sevens at the tally stones. Up the eastern spur to the Shard.

Read in order, the trail shows the old world first and the exiles' passage last; it never contradicts the exodus.

**The founding (CR 3).** The Shard was a name tower, already covered in struck names, already half-abandoned. The exiles hollowed its eastern fragments into workshops, put their scouts on the spur to watch the road, and called the place Mayflower for the May they left in and the wildflowers that filled the valley. The scout encampment is still the front door. Pale dust, copper and tin, limestone, clay, ashbeam and flax gave them everything a craft town needs.

#### Craft halls (lore only)
The **halls of the Eightfold** work like guilds: masters, apprentices, shared stores, a shared voice in town. A work counts as finished only when a second hall has checked it, which is called being **"proved in the Eightfold."** *No guild or hall system exists in game; this is lore and epilogue foreshadowing (§20).*

#### Quiet defiance
- Makers sign with their own mark and the hidden crosswise hammer.
- Mark-books stay under the Strongroom floor. Apprentices memorise them in sevens.
- Forges are banked low. The town's smoke is kept thin enough to pass for a salvage camp.
- The Herald reads the Book of Crowns at the gate every morning; the town listens politely and goes back to work.
- Saying: *"Proved in the Eightfold"* means honestly made. The Crown hears a compliment; makers hear a creed.

#### The Crown's tax
The Kings found Mayflower within a generation. They needed its work, so they taxed it instead of burning it:
- **CR 22, the Tithe of Hands.** One finished work in seven goes up the King's Way to the Citadel.
- **The signing law.** Mayflower may make anything but must sign nothing. Unsigned goods are taxed at a tenth and signed goods at half. The hidden hammer is a hanging offence when found.
- **The Whitening** extends to the forges: every hall's walls must be whitewashed each spring.
- **CR 61, the Levy of Smiths.** The Crown marched a third of Mayflower's smiths to the Slag Graben to work the Old Sill under House Sieveholt. That is why Sieveholt's arms carry a **hammer-head with no haft**, the makers' hammer with nothing to hold it by, and why the Ungraded of the Slagpits still count in sevens (§9).

#### In the finale
Mayflower's makers are the Anvil's true heirs (§19). When the player reaches the Anvil Hall, the makers come up from Pale Hollow with their grandparents' tools and the Carried Marks. They prove each re-cut stone against its original mark the way the Eightfold proves work, then stand vigil over the Ring when the fighting is done.

## 8. Ashlands (Lv 21–40) — Chapter II
Hub: **Obsidia**. Written ahead of content.

**The land.** A rain-shadow basin under the volcanic **Ember Crown**, with the worn shield of the **Ash Rise** to the south-east. The **Smirch** rises from lava-tube springs, braids over iron-sand washes and dies in the **Glass Pan**, a salt flat whose dead brine shore is **the Polished Shore**. The **Pointing Flows** are a fan of glass tongues running down off the Ash Rise. **Deadwood Grove** is a forest turned to stone on the Rise's slope. Crossings: **Smirch Ford**, and **Ashgate Saddle** east toward the Slagpits.

**Obsidia.** Black-glass terraces on a bench at the Smirch's spring line (the only perennial fresh water), glass cliffs at its back. Mirror plazas, ash-shuttered windows. No written signs: directions come by reflection and coloured glass.
- *Official:* raised by the Kings to prove beauty can grow from ruin.
- *Truth:* an older city, renamed. The **Clouded Mirror** won't polish clear and shows a skyline that isn't Obsidia's: the city's old name was **Lanternfall**.

### 8.1 People and customs
- **Obsidians.** Polish is virtue; a smudge is shame. Mirror-priests teach *"the past is a smudge; wipe it."* Children are given a hand mirror at naming and must keep it spotless for life.
- **Saltwalkers.** Pan nomads who read the brine by its colour and travel only at night. They dislike mirrors ("a mirror is a wall that lies") and many are quietly Unwritten.
- **Ash Scholars**. Renegade scholars who take parchment rubbings of name-plates sealed in glass. The Crown calls them a cult.
- **Dialect.** Formal, clipped, lots of reflexive verbs ("one sees oneself to the door"). Insult: *"smudged."* Compliment: *"clear as Pan."*
- **Sayings:** *"Wipe twice, speak once."* · *"Glass remembers heat."* (Saltwalker) · *"Every flow has a tail."* (Ash Scholar; it means "follow it home.")
- **Food.** Salt-baked stone scale fish; ash-roasted roots; black bread leavened with vent steam; **ink-salt** is never eaten (it stains the mouth and marks you as a smuggler). Feast drink: **ember tea**, steeped on a vent stone.
- **Burial.** Crown way: the body is laid on a vent shelf and reduced to ash; the ash is swept into the Glass Pan. Saltwalker way: the dead are laid on the Pan with a mirror face-down on the chest, "so they see where they came from, not where they're going."
- **Festival: Polishing Day.** Every mirror in Obsidia is polished at once at noon. Anyone whose mirror shows a smudge by dusk pays a tithe in gold.

### 8.2 Economy and trade
Iron ore and lapis from the Ember Crown's roots; rock salt and **ink salt** from the Glass Pan; iron sand from the Smirch washes; petrified lumber and ancient branch from Deadwood Grove; shade cotton and ash dust from the bajadas; stone scale fish from the brine ponds. Glassworks fire on vent heat (petrified wood doesn't burn) and pay the Crown in gold and **mirror-tithes**.

**Ink salt is the Crown's strategic export.** Every Scrivener's ink in the Reach is cut with it. The Ashlands literally supply the means of rewriting. Battle Mage Lances guard the pans.

### 8.3 The land's two stories
| Place | Kings' version | Land's truth |
|---|---|---|
| **The Pointing Flows** | The flows came from Obsidia's vents. | Lava only runs downhill. Trace every tail uphill: they converge on the Citadel, where the Burning began. |
| **The Mirror Ring** (at the apex of the fan, on an Ash Rise saddle facing the Massif) | Druid henge of the Ashen Age. | A great ring glazed by the Burning's flare; its reflection shows the Anvil arms with the hammer intact. |
| **Glass-locked tablets** (carried by Obsidian Constructs; rubbings by Ash Scholars) | (no such things) | The Burning order, signed by seven hands. One signature is struck through. Ink can lie, so it's a hint, not proof. |
| **The Clouded Mirror** | A flawed mirror. | Lanternfall's skyline. |
| **Ash Colossus Remnant** | A monster of the false forges. | An Anvil-Warden caught walking *toward* the fire. |

## 9. Slagpits (Lv 41–60) — Chapter III
Hub: **the Great Filter**. Written ahead of content.

**The land.** The **Slag Graben**, a fault-dropped trough between the Ash Rise and the Massif's north-east flank. Entered down the **Chainstair** (switchbacks and rope lifts). The **Grist** comes off the Massif, widens at the hot-spring creeks and the **Sulfur Run**, and leaves south through the **Sieve Gorge**, crossed at **Grade Bridge**. The **Old Sill** terrace stands beside the gorge head.

**The Great Filter.** A vast stepped grate across the gorge head. The Grist's drop drives its stamps and lifts, and heavy ore settles at the break: a real, physical sieve. Every newcomer passes through it and is given a **grade** for life.
- *Official:* the Filter "purifies the realm," separating useful ore and useful people from waste.
- *Truth:* the Anvil's **ore sieve**, where makers graded ore and proved metal before it reached a forge. The Kings turned an ore-sorter into a people-sorter, and in CR 61 staffed it with smiths levied from Mayflower (§7.6).

### 9.1 People and customs
- **Graders** (graded folk). Grade-talk replaces names: "a Third," "a Sill-born," "Ninth-and-rising." Higher grades live higher on the **grade-terraces**, upwind of the sulfur. Pay comes in **gold scrip**, which only spends inside the Graben.
- **The Ungraded.** A hidden camp of refusers who keep their family names and count in sevens: the Unwritten's strongest cell. Many descend from the Mayflower smiths of the Levy of Smiths (CR 61) and still mark their tools with a hidden crosswise hammer.
- **Dialect.** Blunt, fast, sardonic. People rate everything ("that's a Fifth of a sandwich"). Workers greet each other with the number of their shift, never their name.
- **Sayings:** *"Worth is measured — and the measure's bent."* · *"Heavy sinks. So do the honest."* · *"Don't ask what's in the heap."*
- **Food.** Bitter-spice stew, mostly spice; bloodbeam-sap porridge (red and iron-tasting); sulfur-spring eggs. Most food is imported, so meals are rationed by grade. Feast: **Rising Day**, when a worker climbs a grade, shares a whole loaf with the shift.
- **Burial.** Crown way: the dead are graded one last time ("Rendered") and tipped onto the spoil heaps. Ungraded way: they whisper the dead's family name into a hole in the slag and seal it with a hand-shaped plug of clay.

### 9.2 Economy and trade
Mythril and onyx from the fault scarps (Smithing at its peak here); sulfur from the vents; bloodbeam logs along the Grist (red sap from iron-rich water); bitter spice and slag dust on the dry floor. Whoever holds the Filter holds the only way south. Mythril and onyx go up to the Citadel; sulfur goes to Obsidia's glassworks and Bellmarsh's foundries.

|---|---|---|
| **The Great Sort** (CR 112) | Fair, individual grading. | The **Inspector's Book** shows entire towns marked "waste" in one day, each grading signed by six Houses. The seventh column is blank. |
| **Slag-sealed homes** | Waste ground, never settled. | Dig in: furnished pre-Crown houses, whole villages moved from Seam Country and the Ashlands and buried. |
| **The Reverse Grate** | A broken tier. | Run backward, it turns up old proving-tags, including one for a Scrivener "graded out of the Book." |
| **The Sieve Ring** (under the largest heap on the Old Sill, beneath a sealed village) | No such ring. | The Filter's original verification core. |
| **Filter Wardens** | Loyal servants beyond death. | Undead graders still sorting ore and people nobody brings them. |

## 10. Bellmarsh (Lv 61–80) — Chapter IV
Hub: **Belltower Keep**. Written ahead of content.

**The land.** The Grist (from Sieve Gorge) and the larger **Tolle** (from the Massif) meet in the **Bell Mere**, a broad, shallow lake and fen that spills over a bedrock sill into the **Tolle Delta** and on to the **Tolle Sound**. The **Deep Pools**, scour holes above the sill, are the mere's deepest water. The **Tollspine** foothills wall the west, with limestone caves. Crossings: beach-ridge causeways, **Hanne's ferry** over the deep channel, and **Toll Bridge** north to the Throne Approach.

**Belltower Keep.** A fortress of nested belltowers on a granite tor near the confluence: the only flood-safe bedrock for miles. Bells ring every hour and at every death, and their sound carries over the flat water.

**The sea.** The Tolle Sound is the Reach's only usable sea outlet. The **Grey Sea** behind the Westwall is real but unreachable: sheer cliff coast, no harbour. Crown cargo can leave by the delta, but only under the Keep's bells.

### 10.1 People and customs
- **Marshfolk.** Quiet, devout, sign-speaking. Speaking during a toll is taboo, because the bells once drowned out screams. The Keep sells **Bell Rights**: gold paid to have a bell rung in your name.
- **Ferry families**, led by **Hanne the Ferrywoman**: descendants of the drowned. Hanne ferries the dead and refuses to cross one sunken street.
- **The Silent Choir.** Women who sign the drowned city's prayers during tolls.
- **Dialect.** Spoken Marsh is soft and full of water words; **Tollsign** (hand-speech) is richer than speech. The sign for "remember" is a hand pressed flat on the water.
- **Sayings:** *"Between bells, say it quick."* · *"The mere keeps what the Keep forgets."* · *"Ring for me, and I'll be gone."*
- **Food.** Smoked echo carp; glass eel jelly; bellwood-bark tea; marsh rice cakes. **Bell koi** is forbidden to commoners. Poaching it is the marsh's most common crime and its favourite feast.
- **Burial.** Crown way: a bell rung once, the body sent down the delta on a reed raft "into the Quiet." Old way: the name is whispered into the water between tolls; ferry families lay the dead in the shallows over the sunken streets "so they're home."

### 10.2 Economy and trade
Darksteel and jadeite from the Tollspine foothills; bellwood, bark and **bell dust** from the swamp forest; echo carp and glass eel in the channels; bell koi only in the Deep Pools. Ferrying is a trade of its own. All tribute bound for the Citadel passes the Keep.

### 10.3 The land's two stories
| Place | Kings' version | Land's truth |
|---|---|---|
| **The Bell Mere** | "Always a marsh." | **The Drowning** (CR 141). The **Sluice ruins** on the sill were plainly built and plainly shut, drowning the Unwritten city of **Undertoll**. One lake drained (Pale Hollow), one valley drowned. |
| **The bells** | They keep away drowning spirits. | Cast from melted forge-stones. **The bell-founders' recipe:** "bell dust, 3 parts; forge-stone, 7 parts." Their ringing masks the Anvil's hum. |
| **The Silent Bell** | A cracked, useless bell. | Its silence lets you hear Undertoll. |
| **The Drowned Ring** (Deep Pools, on foot only at low sluice) | No ring. | Undertoll's town-square ring. It wakes only while the Silent Bell is silent. |
| **Echo Wraiths** | Drowning spirits. | The drowned. Their echo extract replays sentences from Undertoll, including one about "the one who kept the hammer." |

## 11. Throne Approach (Lv 81–100) — Chapter V
Hub: **the Citadel of the Seven Seats**, the Crown's name for the seven-tower core of **Anvilhold**, the fallen ancient city of the makers. Its heart is the Anvil Hall. Written ahead of content.

> **Design note.** Anvilhold is designed as an *instanced dungeon*. Nothing about instancing exists in code. This section is lore and design intent only.

**The land.** The **Crown Massif**, the Reach's water divide. The Tolle, the Grist and the Smirch's southern washes rise within a day's walk of the Citadel; Pale Hollow does not (it is a closed basin). The only way in is from the south: Toll Bridge, then the **King's Stair**, switchbacks beside the Tolle's cataracts. Approach Wood gives way to alpine heath. The city sits on a plateau shelf over a kimberlite pipe, whose resistant neck, **the Anvil Neck**, is the cracked first Anvil. Palace Ruins terraces step down the plateau edge.

### 11.1 Anvilhold: the fallen city
**Its rise.** In the Age of the Anvil, Anvilhold was the greatest makers' city in the Reach, built in rings around the first Anvil.

**Its fall.**
- **CR 0–3: the Exodus.** Its makers fled to found Mayflower (§7.6). The Kings built seven towers around the Anvil Hall and ruled from a city emptied of its hands.
- **CR 3–220: the long hollowing.** With no makers, the lower city decayed. The Crown filled it with Scriveners, courtiers and Crownwardens and called it the Citadel.
- **CR 221: the Withdrawal.** The Six sealed themselves inside the Anvil Hall. The court that remained turned devout, then fanatical, and became the **Cultists of the Seven** (§11.3). Ordinary courtiers and Crownwardens moved down the King's Stair to the checkpoint stations. The upper city has belonged to the cult ever since.

**Where the Kings rule now: hidden behind the cult.** The six New Kings still rule from the sealed **Anvil Hall** at the heart of the ruin, on the Seven-Seat Throne around the Anvil Neck. No one but the cult's six **Seat-Keepers** passes the Hall's doors. Edicts come out through the Seat-Keepers to the **Throne Seneschal**, who keeps a court at the foot of the city gate and sends orders down the King's Way. The rest of the Reach doesn't know the Citadel is a ruin full of zealots. The Book of Crowns still calls it "the shining Seat."

### 11.2 The dungeon, as story
The player enters Anvilhold as a dungeon: a descent through the makers' city to the Anvil at its heart. Each district is a chapter of what was lost.

| # | District | What's there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Gate of Six Crowns** | The city gate, with six crowns on poles and one empty pole. The Throne Seneschal's court sits outside it. Inside, the cult's first Penitents whitewash the walls endlessly, and the whitewash is a hundred layers thick. |
| 2 | **The Cold Forges** | The old smithing quarter: rows of empty forges, cold for three hundred years. Anvils are still in place; tool racks are empty, because everyone took their tools in the Exodus. Bellows rotted to leather rags. ### 11.3 The Cultists of the Seven
**Who they are.** The remnant court of the Citadel, turned fanatic after the Withdrawal: Scriveners, courtiers and Crownwarden families who stayed with the Seats. They believe the Kings' crowned word is not a decree but **the world itself**: what the Crown speaks *is*, and whatever goes unspoken and forgotten never lived.

- **Creed:** *"Seven came through ash. The crowned word is the only word. What is unspoken was never born; what is forgotten never lived."*
- **What they guard:** the Anvil Hall, the six Seats, the sealed Kings, the Scriptorium's master Book and the cracked Anvil itself. Above all they guard against the Hammer being lifted.
- **The twisted Seventh Seat.** To the cult, the empty Seat is not a lie to hide but a **promise**. They teach that a **Seventh King** will one day be crowned to sit it, and when all seven Seats are full the Book will be *finished*: nothing will ever be rewritten again, because nothing will remain that disagrees. They prepare the Seat with offerings of the erased: names chiselled off stones and people the cult has "corrected." Their champion, the **Seventh Claimant**, whom the cult calls **the Correction**, believes he is that King.

**Ranks.**
1. **Penitents**: novices who whitewash stones, every carved name and mark they can reach, until the stone forgets. Grey robes, hands stained white.
2. **Copy-Brothers / Copy-Sisters**: copy the Book of Crowns by hand, endlessly, in fading ink-salt ink.
3. **The Ink-Sworn**: Correctors turned inquisitors. They "correct" heretics by reading their lives aloud and re-writing them. Black-stained to the elbow.
4. **The Crowned Penitents**: elite guards who wear iron crowns welded shut over their eyes, "so they see only the Book."
5. **The Seat-Keepers**: six high priests, one per House, the only ones who pass the Anvil Hall's doors.
6. **The Seventh Claimant**: the cult's chosen King-to-be, called **the Correction**.

**The naming rite: Taking the Ink.** No cultist keeps the name they were born with. On the night a novice is accepted, an Ink-Sworn reads the novice's birth name aloud from the Book one last time, then **strikes it through** with a single line of ink-salt ink and writes the cult name beside it. From that moment the birth name "was not." Saying it aloud is a heresy, and answering to it is worse. The struck line is kept, not scraped: the cult believes a name crossed out in the Book is *more* erased than one never written, because the Book has witnessed its death.
- **Who names whom.** Penitents are named by the Ink-Sworn who corrected them, after their worst sin or their first task. Copy-Brothers and Copy-Sisters take a scriptorium name from their tools or their damage. The Ink-Sworn name themselves after the ink. A Crowned Penitent is renamed a second time, at the welding of the crown. Seat-Keepers are named by the other five, and the name is cut into their Seat.
- **The ink.** The novice's hands are pressed into the inkwell up to the wrist. Penitents' stains are later whitewashed over; the Ink-Sworn let theirs climb to the elbow.
- **What survives.** Stone is true and ink can lie. The struck names still sit in the Book under a single line, legible to anyone who reads crossed-out words. Families in Mayflower and the hubs also remember the people the cult took. A struck name found and spoken back is a **struck name**: a world fragment of the "ink" type that the player can recover from torn pages of the Book, confession sheets, Seat carvings and the Carried Marks.

**Cult names by rank.** Grim on purpose: each name is a wound, a tool or a sin.
- **Penitents:** Hollow, Ashmouth, Nailbitten, Unwept, Sootkin, Gristle, Lime-Hand, Ninth-Lash, Knucklebare, Dregs, Saltsore, Mute Wick.
- **Copy-Brothers and Copy-Sisters:** Brother Gallowquill, Sister Rot-Vellum, Brother Scour, Sister Lampblack, Brother Seamflay, Sister Pumice, Brother Blotmarrow, Sister Gall-Ink, Brother Penknife, Sister Wormhole, Brother Erasure, Sister Margin-Bone.
- **Ink-Sworn:** Blackwater, Gall-and-Thorn, Cinderwick, the Blind Hand, Iron-Gall, Soot-and-Vinegar, the Last Line, Quenchmark, Oakgall, the Red Rubric, Drowned Quill, the Nib.
- **Crowned Penitents:** the Weld, Rivet-Eyes, Sightless Ward, Iron Hood, the Shuttered, Crownscar, Blinder, the Lidless Gate, Seven-Nails, Rust-Halo, the Dark Visor, Grey Coronet.

**What happened to the Seat-Keepers.** Each of the six was once a named official of the Citadel, and each took the ink a second time at the Withdrawal (CR 221), when the Seats were sealed. Their birth names were struck through in the Book. Each name survives only as a **struck name** the player can recover in the world (§11.4).

**Rites.**
- **The Recitation**: the Kings' crowned names chanted around the clock, in shifts, so that nothing else is spoken and the old names stay forgotten.
- **The Striking-Out**: a heretic's life read aloud and rewritten and their name struck through; the struck-out are given new names and made Penitents, or burned and offered to the Seventh Seat.
- **The Whitening Without End**: cult walls are whitewashed every day.
- **The Silence of Six**: the word "six" is never spoken. Members count *one, two, three, four, five, seven*.
- **The Crowning Vigil**: once a year the Claimant sits beside the Seventh Seat until dawn, waiting to be accepted.

**Cult heresies (by their law).**
- **The Hammer heresy**: that the Hammer belongs to anyone. Worst of all crimes.
- **Stone-reading**: trusting struck stone over the Book.
- **Counting in sevens** like the Unwritten (as opposed to the cult's six-skipping count).
- **The Exodus**: the cult teaches the makers never existed; Mayflower is "a salvage camp."

## 13. Time Mages and the warp rings

### 13.1 Time Mages
The only discipline that perceives the past directly: a Time Mage sees a thing *as it was first made or struck*. In the Age of the Anvil they were the **Keepers of the Glass**, who tuned the rings' song. The six Scriveners needed them gone. The **Glass Purge** (CR 4–19) hunted them; the Book of Crowns says the last were burned. They weren't all. Shard Wights are the purged dead. The living hide in plain sight: the Pale Hollow Trainer is one.

**Crown law:** Time Magic is "an extinct heresy." In the game, the job is playable and openly trained. Lore frame: the Crown's Trainers teach a hollowed-out official version, "Chronomancy," as a battlefield trick, stripped of seeing. A player who reads the fragments learns what the job was really for.

### 13.2 What the rings are
Rings of seven to nine leaning rune-monoliths on sunken stone platforms, half-swallowed by forest, ash or water. They are surviving forge-stones of the Anvil. Before the Crown, the rings sang to one another through the Anvil's hum, and travellers learned to ride the song from circle to circle.

- **Great rings (5):** one per region, each with its own keepers in the old world. Named below.
- **Lesser circles:** waystones that carried the great rings' song. Seam Country has eight (§7.5); more lie unfound in other regions. They wake only when the great ring of their region is woken.

**How they were silenced.** In the Unstriking, the Scriveners re-cut one rune per stone so each circle's song went sour. A ring out of tune cannot open. The Book of Crowns calls them "druid henges of the Ashen Age" and forbids gathering inside them. Crownwardens topple any stone that glows.

| Region | Great ring | Where | Buried truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pale Hollow | **The Chalk Ring** | A sunken rune-floor at the old crossroads, labelled **The Sheepfold** on Crown maps (84,216) | The Shard was a name tower; the Wights hold the true runes. |
| Ashlands | **The Mirror Ring** | Apex of the Pointing Flows, on an Ash Rise saddle facing the Massif | Glazed by the Burning; its reflection shows the hammer intact. |
| Slagpits | **The Sieve Ring** | Under the largest heap on the Old Sill, beneath a sealed village | The Filter's verification core; wakes the Inspector's Book. |
| Bellmarsh | **The Drowned Ring** | The Deep Pools, on foot only at low sluice | The bells were cast from its missing stones; it wakes only while the Silent Bell is silent. |
| Throne Approach | **The Seven-Stone Ring** | The Anvil Hall, around the Anvil Neck | Six of seven stones re-cut into the Kings' House marks; the seventh is plain, with the hammer laid crosswise. |

The **Scarp Head Ring** in Seam Country is a lesser circle whose north face was fused to glass by the same flare that glazed the Mirror Ring. It foreshadows the Ashlands.

### 13.3 Reading and waking
- **Anyone can read a fragment.** Struck stone speaks to anyone: any job can read a fragment once the one before it has been read (each fragment needs the one before it).
- **Waking a great ring** happens one region at a time, in campaign order:
  1. Uncover the region's buried truth (ruins, writings, witnesses).
  2. Recover the original rune for each re-cut stone, from a carving, a witness, or a laid-to-rest Wight.
  3. Sing the ring back into agreement. A Time Mage sees the original runes under the cuts and leads the singing; other jobs carry the recovered runes and strike them. Every job can take part; Time Mages have the special role of reading what was cut away.
  4. The ring wakes and links to every ring already woken. The Kings feel each waking; Crownwarden pressure rises in the next region.
- **When a ring wakes, that region's Anvil's Sparks burn a little brighter** — a lore note people talk about, not a render commitment.
- **Gameplay status:** the rings are physical, dormant landmarks. Nothing is wired. Any future gameplay role (for instance, travel between woken rings) is undecided.

## 14. The sky, timekeeping and script

### 14.1 The sky
The in-game pixel sky (first- and third-person views; it follows the server's day/night cycle and weather) is canon, and is read two ways:
- **The galaxy band.** Struck Faith: **the Anvil's Sparks**, the sparks of the first strike, and in the Burning more sparks flew up from the cracked Anvil. Each star is a deed or a name the Kings could not reach: they could whitewash walls, never the sky. Crown: **the Ash Veil**, smoke of the false forges.
- **The Turning Wheels.** Three spiral swirls: the Keeper of the Glass's hourglasses, the patterns Time Mages read. The Crown forbids naming them.
- **Falling strikes.** Shooting stars: a lost name coming home. Folk custom is to say a dead person's name when one falls. That's illegal under the Crown Rite, so people whisper.
- **Sun and moon.** The Crown calls the sun **the Crown's Eye** and dates the day from noon. The old count names the moon **the Witness**, because it watches when the names of the dead are spoken.
- **Weather.** Overcast and fog that hide the stars are **Crown weather**: "the Kings are hiding the sky again."

### 14.2 Timekeeping
- **Crown Reckoning (CR).** Year 0 is the Coronation. The present is CR 300.
- **The Crown Day** lasts 72 minutes of real time. Bells toll at dawn and dusk. The galaxy rises as night deepens; Unwritten meetings happen under it.
- **Anvil-reckoning.** The old count, in sevens: tallies, the knock, ring runes. A "full count" is 49 (seven sevens); old leases ran 49 years.
- **The Crown week** has ten days (the Crown changed it). Unwritten households secretly keep a seven-day **strike-week**, resting on the seventh, "the Hammer's day."

### 14.3 Script
- **Strike Runes.** Angular cuts made to be struck into stone. Found on forge-stones, rings and fragments.
- **Crown Script.** Rounded formal letters for official text, derived from Strike Runes with the corners deliberately softened. A Scrivener's joke: "we sanded the truth smooth."
- **Tollsign.** Bellmarsh hand-speech.
- **Ink.** Scriveners' ink is cut with Ashlands ink salt. It fades in about forty years, which is why the Book of Crowns is always being recopied, and why nothing in ink is as old as the stones.

## 15. Gods, saints, heresies and the afterlife

### 15.1 The Struck Faith (suppressed)
No god-king. The holy figures are the **Smith** (who struck first and left the Hammer), the **Witness** (the moon, who remembers) and the **Keeper of the Glass** (the hourglasses, who keeps time in order). The faith's centre is the **Witness-rite**: every true deed, once struck, lives in the Anvil and is honoured by being remembered.
- **Rites:** striking a family's names into stone; the knock (three short, one long); "keeping a song" (memorising your ancestors); the chalk circle drawn and rubbed out.
- **Sacred texts:** none written; the stones themselves. The **Scriveners' Oath** (the Twelfth Fragment) is its holiest found text.
- **Shrines:** the Whitewashed Chapel, Hollow Stair Top, Scarp Cave Shrine.

### 15.2 The Crown Rite (official)
The Kings aren't called gods. They're **the Hands of Order**, chosen by "the Flame That Was Quenched." People worship *order itself*: obedience, the Toll, the corrected history. Rites: the Whitening, reading the Book of Crowns on rest-days, bell tolls for the dead. Clergy are licensed Clerics. Sacred text: the Book of Crowns, opening with the *Book of Ash*. Holy sign: the seven-pointed crown.

### 15.3 Saints
| Saint | Faith | Story | Feast / sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **St. Hesk the Unwritten** | Struck (secret) | The seventh Scrivener, who refused and left the Hammer open. | A hammer drawn crosswise and rubbed out. |
| **The Keeper Who Stayed** | Struck | A Time Mage who sat in the Chalk Ring through the Glass Purge, holding its runes in her head, until the Crownwardens walled her into the Shard. | An hourglass with one grain stuck. |
| **The Keeper of the Stillmere** | Struck (Seam Country) | Buried the sunk hall's dead facing the water so they could see home. | Graves turned toward water. |
| **St. Corrin of the Clean Page** | Crown | The first Corrector, who "freed" the Shard of its "false carvings." | Whitewash brush. Patron of the Whitening. |
| **St. Aubre the Measured** | Crown | First Inspector of the Filter. | A sieve. Patron of graders. |
| **The Bell-Mother** | Crown (Bellmarsh) | Cast the first bell "to keep out the drowning." | A bell. Patron of the Keep. |

### 15.4 Heresies
- **Stone-worship**: the Crown's name for the Struck Faith.
- **Chronomancy of seeing**: using Time Magic to perceive the past. Punishable by Retirement.
- **The Six-Count**: the claim that there are only six Kings. Saying "six" in the Citadel is treason.
- **The Whitened** (a Crown-side heresy): believe forgetting itself is holy and whitewash their own skin. The Crown finds them embarrassing and useful.
- **The Unstruck Heresy**: a fringe Struck belief that forgotten people never existed at all. The Unwritten reject it; it's what the Kings would want.

### 15.5 Afterlife
- **Crown:** the dead go into **the Quiet**, a peaceful forgetting. Bells ring them in; their names are retired from the Book of Crowns. Being forgotten is a mercy.
- **Struck Faith:** the dead endure as long as their deeds are struck and remembered. A person whose name is erased is **unmade**, neither at rest nor gone. This is why the undead rise (§12.1).

## 19. The campaign: five chapters

### Chapter I — *The Whitening* (Pale Hollow and Seam Country, Lv 1–20)
The player arrives at the Shard Dwellings in Whitening season and takes Crown service from the Herald: kill quests in the yards. The Shard Elder tells the Crown Spire story. The Humming Stone hums. At Crestcut Knoll the player reads the first fragment and starts following the sevens: moorings, a burned census, the old road. In the Shard Crypt the Crypt Watch sends them to "Shut the Mouth"; laying the Wights to rest gives up the Chalk Ring's true runes. North over the Old Saddle, Seam Country's lesser circles line up on one bearing. In the Scarp Cave the player finds the Scriveners' Oath and learns there were seven hands, six now under crowns, and a seventh that **left the hammer open**. The Chalk Ring wakes. Somewhere far away, a crown turns on its pole.
**Chapter fragments:** the First to the Thirteenth Fragment. **Beat:** the hammer is named.

### Chapter II — *The Polished City* (Ashlands, Lv 21–40)
Obsidia polishes everything. The player follows the Pointing Flows uphill and finds they all point at the Citadel, not the vents. Ash Scholars' rubbings and Obsidian Constructs' glass-locked tablets give the Burning order, "signed by seven hands," one signature struck through (ink: a hint, not proof). Saro the Blind Glazier remembers Lanternfall. At the Mirror Ring the reflection shows the Anvil arms with the hammer intact. The ring wakes.
**Beat:** the Kings started the Burning; one of them refused to sign.

### Chapter III — *The Sort* (Slagpits, Lv 41–60)
The player is graded on arrival. The Ungraded take them in. The Inspector's Book shows whole towns marked "waste," signed by six Houses. The Reverse Grate prints a Scrivener "graded out of the Book." Under a sealed village on the Old Sill, the Sieve Ring re-checks the Book and the seventh column comes back blank: not a missing signature but a refusal. The ring wakes.
**Beat:** the seventh never signed anything. The Kings erased one of their own.

### Chapter IV — *Between Bells* (Bellmarsh, Lv 61–80)
Speech stops at every toll. Hanne ferries the player as far as the sunken street. The bell-founders' recipe counts forge-stone in sevens. When the Silent Bell is silenced and the sluice is lowered, the Drowned Ring rises in the Deep Pools. An Echo Wraith replays a line about "the one who kept the hammer." Undertoll was where Hesk's people lived. The Kings drowned them. The ring wakes, and the bells across the marsh miss a beat.
**Beat:** the seventh's name, **Hesk**, and what it cost.

### Chapter V — *Struck Once* (Throne Approach, Lv 81–100)
Up the King's Stair, past Petrified Guards, to the Throne Seneschal's court at the gate. Behind it, the Citadel the Book of Crowns calls "the shining Seat" turns out to be **Anvilhold**: a dead makers' city overrun by the **Cultists of the Seven**. The player descends through the Cold Forges, where every anvil bears a scraped hammer, and down Markwright's Lane and the Hall of Proving. Along the Avenue of Re-headed Statues, the cult kneels to the bare neck. In the Scriptorium of Corrections the seventh row is blank in both *was* and *shall be*. In the cult's deepest cell, the Last Unwritten recites the motto and knocks. Ilse Morrowglass, Mayflower's Trainer, arrives with an hourglass and admits what she is. The six Seat-Keepers fall tower by tower: the Gutted Archive, Mother Tithe-of-Teeth, the Flayed Saint, the Sieve of Bones, the Abbess of the Drowned Bell and the Lance Unforgiven. In each tower the player can recover the Keeper's struck birth name.

**The finale, in the Anvil Hall.** Behind the sealed doors the Seven-Seat Throne stands in a ring around the cracked first Anvil, inside the Seven-Stone Ring. The **Correction**, the Seventh Claimant, waits beside the empty Seat. Six stones carry the Kings' House marks. The seventh is plain, white like House Unnamed's arms, and its rune is the **hammer laid crosswise**: the open strike scraped from every defaced Anvil since Crestcut Knoll. The Seventh Seat was never a throne. It belonged to the open strike, which means anyone's hand.

The **strike** is shared. Mayflower's makers come up the King's Stair with the Carried Marks and their grandparents' hammers and prove each re-cut stone (§7.6). A Time Mage reads the six original runes under the House marks and leads the ring's singing. Every other job strikes the recovered runes, and any job may take up the Hammer. When the player strikes the Anvil, it rings for the first time in three hundred years. Every woken ring answers, and across the Reach every chiselled-off name sounds once in the stone it was cut from, so that the erased dead hear themselves called and can finally rest. **The Anvil's Sparks flood the night sky.** The overcut motto *"Struck once, by the Crown"* cracks back to the original:

> ***Struck once, true for all.***

It is the old smith's oath, the words every Anvilhold maker swore over honest work, and now it is spoken over a name. The Hammer gives back what the chisel took.

The six Seats are found empty except for six crowns. The Kings are never seen. Whether they fled, died, or were only ever ink is never answered. The cult breaks; the Correction, whose name was cut out rather than struck, is given back his mark from the Carried Marks and remembers he was a Mayflower child.

**After.** A name restored is not a war won. The Kings' servants don't vanish: the Seneschal's court, the Scriveners and their fire push back, and the Ring has to be guarded. Mayflower's makers rekindle the Cold Forges and keep watch beside it. In game terms, clearing the Citadel ends a run, and afterwards field enemies grow stronger to match the player (up to 1.75×): *the Kings push back; the Ring must be held.*

**Supporting reveals (Candidates B and C, folded in):**
- *The Kings are only ink* (B, never confirmed): faceless crowns on poles, sealed Seats nobody but the cult has seen, mirror-priests who won't show the Kings' reflections, Bell Rights rung for Kings with no names. The bible never resolves whether the Six were still people.
- *The sky remembers* (C, the finale's visual): the Anvil's Sparks were always the names the Kings could not reach; that's why the Crown named them the Ash Veil.

## 20. Epilogue (foreshadowing only)
The Anvil was never meant for one hand. After the finale, the Last Unwritten walks free into the sun for the first time in forty years, says his mother's name aloud, and weeps.

The epilogue only hints at what comes next, and nothing here is built:
- Small bands of adventurers begin meeting at woken rings: **guilds**, newly re-forming.
- In Mayflower the first maker in three hundred years signs a masterwork openly, with the crosswise hammer on top.
- Under the next falling star, whole towns say their dead's names out loud.
- The Seventh Seat stays empty, and the cult's remnants are still out there.

## 23. Writing tone guide
- **Quiet dread under bright pixels.** The world looks cozy; the truth is in the cracks.
- **Show contradictions, not lectures.** Pair an official plaque with a ruin that disagrees. Let players connect them.
- **Concrete over abstract.** A birth carving, a bell recipe, a terrace keeper's tallies, not "ancient power."
- **Officials speak in polished certainty** ("It has always been so"). **Witnesses speak in fragments.**
- **Short lines.** Item and NPC text two sentences at most where possible.
- **Dry humour** from salvagers and Slagpit workers. No memes, no fourth-wall breaks.
- **Gold is the currency.** The Citadel's code display name never appears in prose.
- **Guilds** appear only as an epilogue hint.
- **Recurring motifs:** sevens, the crosswise hammer, whitewash, mirrors, sieves, bells, crowns on poles, the hum.

## Appendix A. Canon decisions
1. **One story.** Legacy and earlier-draft lore is dropped. The conflict is inside the world: the Kings' stolen history against what the land remembers.
2. **Citadel name.** Prose: **the Citadel of the Seven Seats**, the seven-tower core of fallen **Anvilhold** (see 20). The game's code name has no in-world meaning and no inscription fragment.
3. **Seventh House.** The seventh Scrivener, **Hesk Tallowmere** of House Tallowmere, refused the Revision and the crown and became the first Unwritten. Only six Kings were crowned; the Book of Crowns claims seven (§3.1, §4).
4. **Seven-Stone Ring.** Six of seven stones re-cut into House marks; the seventh plain with the hammer laid crosswise. The Ring is in Throne Approach, in the Anvil Hall around the Anvil Neck.
5. **Finale.** "Struck once, true for all" (Candidate A) is canon; Candidate C is its visual; Candidate B stays as unresolved ambiguity.
6. **After the finale** the Kings' servants push back and the Ring must be guarded (post-finale enemy scaling).
7. **Fragments:** any job can read one, in order.
8. **The finale strike:** every job takes part; Time Mages lead by reading the cut runes.
9. **Ring-waking and the sky:** the region's Sparks brighten. Lore only; no render commitment.
